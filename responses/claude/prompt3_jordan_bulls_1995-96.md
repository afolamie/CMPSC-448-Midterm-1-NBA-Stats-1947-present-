# Prompt 3: Michael Jordan's role on the 1995-96 Chicago Bulls

Prompt: "Analyze Michael Jordan's role on the Chicago Bulls during the 1995-96 NBA regular season using the supplied advanced statistics for Jordan and his teammates."

**Workload and usage.** Jordan played all 82 games and led the team with 3,090 minutes, about 16% of all minutes played by the 15 Bulls in the data. His usage rate of 33.3% was far above any teammate: Pippen was next at 24.4%, followed by Kukoc at 21.4%. No one else on the roster came close to that share of possessions.

**Production.** Jordan was the team's best player on every summary metric. His PER was 29.4, and he generated 20.4 Win Shares (.317 per 48 minutes), more than a quarter of the Bulls' roughly 75 combined Win Shares. His BPM of 10.5 and VORP of 9.8 were the highest on the team, and his OBPM of 8.3 shows that the value was mostly offensive, though his DBPM of 2.2 and 6.2 defensive win shares were also team-best. His true shooting percentage was .582 despite the heavy workload, and he rarely turned the ball over (8.4% turnover rate for a high-usage player).

**Supporting cast.** Scottie Pippen was a clear second option (PER 21.0, 12.3 WS, VORP 5.9) and the best playmaker after Jordan, with a 25.2 assist percentage and strong rebounding. Toni Kukoc was a versatile third scorer (PER 20.4, TS% .589, 10.1 WS). Dennis Rodman contributed almost entirely through rebounding, with a 26.6 total rebound percentage and 33.2 defensive rebound percentage, while his usage was only 10.3%. Steve Kerr was the specialist, with .663 TS% and a .492 three-point attempt rate. Together, Jordan, Pippen and Kukoc produced about 57% of the team's Win Shares.

**Role summary.** Jordan functioned as the primary scorer and offensive engine, carrying a much higher possession share than anyone else while staying efficient. The roster around him was built to complement that role: a second creator in Pippen, a rebounding specialist in Rodman, a floor-spacer in Kerr, and a thin bottom of the bench (Edwards, Caffey, Haley) with negative impact in limited minutes.
